FAQ · INTERNAL AUDIT

Frequently asked questions about internal audit

01

It independently reviews how your business processes and controls operate, identifies risks and their root cause, and proposes remediation plans. It acts as a strategic partner to management for making better business decisions.

02

External audit gives an opinion on whether the financial statements are fairly presented, usually once a year. Internal audit reviews operational and administrative processes and internal controls throughout the year, finds the cause of problems and helps fix them.

03

It is an end-to-end review of core processes such as inventory, sales and A/R, disbursements and A/P, fixed assets and payroll, comparing how they really work against what your policies and controls say and evaluating their performance.

04

It is the set of policies, controls, roles and culture that makes your business operate the way it should, reducing and mitigating risk.

05

Review your key controls before the auditor does: identify deviations, fix what’s critical first and document calculations, business decisions, owners and policies. Arriving prepared means fewer surprises and findings.

06

Address the root cause, not the symptom: understand why it happened, define a plan with owners and dates, implement the control and verify it works before the next audit.

07

Identify your main risks, assign each one a clear control, separate incompatible duties, document applicable policies, make sure they’re followed on the floor, not just on paper, and track your business metrics.

08

Map how each process works today, find where money or time is lost, remove steps that add no value and automate what’s repetitive. Start with what has the biggest impact.
